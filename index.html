Austin Bike Comfort Routes

Pick a start and an end. The route keeps to streets and trails the City of Austin rates comfortable for bicycling, crossing neighborhoods on quiet unrated streets and using a Low-rated street only when nothing else connects.

        • The rating is a city planner's judgment of how comfortable a street is to ride, not a measured risk. On the city's own figures, streets rated High recorded more police-reported crashes per mile than Low ones, probably because more people ride them; nothing here counts riders.
        • The High-Injury Network is the city's Vision Zero list of the streets where the most people have been killed or seriously hurt in traffic, by any mode (austintexas.gov/visionzero). "Exposed" on this page means the rule above, nothing more: a count and the street's layout, not a measure of how likely a crash is. The red rings and the hatched casing mark them; tap one for the numbers. Under each street in the cue sheet: the bike facility, the posted speed, the High-Injury Network flag and the crashes within 60 ft of the line since 2021.
        • Tap a red ring or a hatched stretch and choose "Route around this": the route is planned again with that intersection (every street piece that meets it) or that stretch costing 50 times as much to ride as before. Nothing is removed, so if no other way connects, the route still goes through it and the card says so. The avoided spot is drawn crossed out, the card says how many miles the way around adds, and the choice is kept in the link and on this device. "Avoid hotspots" does the same for every high-crash intersection and exposed stretch in the city at once, on top of Comfort first, and says how many of the spots the comfort route passes it gets around and what that adds. It is not the default: crash counts follow how many people ride a street, and the default stays the city's ratings.
        • Every high-crash intersection and exposed stretch on the route also gets its own row in the cue sheet, marked in red, just ahead of the street it is on ("Ahead: …"). With the follow-me dot on, the page measures how far along the route you are and, about 500 ft before each of them, shows a banner across the top of the map and says it aloud; each one is said once per ride (turning the dot off and on starts a new ride). The "Speak warnings" switch turns the voice off; the banner stays. Speech needs a tap first on an iPhone, and the follow button is that tap. More than 250 ft from the line, the page treats you as off the route and stays quiet. The phone must stay awake with the page open; it does not speak from a locked screen.
        • Each street in the directions starts with the turn onto it, from the angle between the two lines: under 25 degrees is "Continue onto", under 65 "Bear left" or "Bear right onto", 155 and over "Sharp left" or "Sharp right onto"; the first line says which way to head. The angles come from the drawn lines, so a curved corner can read as a bear where the ground says turn. The GPX carries the same turns as a route (rte) for apps that read them, with the exact line as a track.
        • Riding with the page (on a hosted copy over HTTPS, with the follow-me dot on): a strip at the top of the map names the next turn and the distance to it, and each turn is said aloud once about 300 ft before it ("In 300 feet, turn left onto Speedway"); the end is announced the same way. If you are more than 250 ft from the line on three fixes in a row, the route is planned again from where you are to the same end, with the same setting and avoided spots, and the page says so; it does this at most once every 30 seconds and not while the phone's fix is poorer than 60 m. The "Speak" switch covers turns and warnings alike. The phone must stay awake with the page open; it does not speak from a locked screen. Closures and construction are not in the data: the page cannot know a path is blocked until you leave the line.
        • Riders' notes. Anyone this page is shared with as a Contributor or above can mark a street Prefer or Avoid, from the buttons under each street in the directions or with "Note a street" and a tap on the map. A note covers that street within roughly half a mile of where it was marked. When at least 3 more riders prefer a street than avoid it, the planner treats it as a little more comfortable than the city's rating (half the rating's penalty); when at least 3 more avoid it, as a little less (the penalty goes up by 1.5, which puts a Medium street between an unrated collector and a Low street). The city's rating stays the base, "Shortest" ignores the notes, and each rider counts once per street. Everyone the page is shared with sees the counts, not who wrote them.
        • Streets the city has not rated are drawn faint. The planner crosses them when they are local streets and avoids them when they are arterials or highways, but it knows nothing about their lanes, parking or traffic.
        • "Comfort first" will ride a long way round to stay off a Low street; "Balanced" halves that pull; "Shortest" ignores the ratings and shows what the detour costs.
        • One-way streets are respected except where a two-way protected lane or a trail runs. Hills, lights, construction and closures are not in the data.
        • "Start from my location" and the follow button (the ring under the zoom buttons) use the phone's GPS. They work when this page is opened as a normal web page over HTTPS; inside the Claude artifact viewer they are hidden, because the viewer does not allow location.
        • "Open in Google Maps" hands Google the start, the end and up to nine points along this line. Google draws its own bike route between those points, so check it against the line here. On a phone, Google may use only the first three points.
        • Addresses come from the house-number ranges in the city's street file, placed along the block. A number the file does not hold lands on the nearest block of that street.
        • Where a trail meets a street with no junction in the city's file, the two were joined if the trail ends within 20 m of the street's centerline (6 m for a street end). A few joins may not exist on the ground.

        The city's own map is at austintexas.gov/bikemap. The ratings, crashes and gaps are on the companion comfort map.
